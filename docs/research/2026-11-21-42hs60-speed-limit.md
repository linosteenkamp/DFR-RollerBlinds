# Research: the speed limit and the driver settings of the 42HS60-1504JA05-D20-03

Ticket: GitHub issue #25 (part of map #14, the gearbox test rig).
Researched on 2026-11-21. This document uses the same method and the
same table layout as "Research: the speed limit of the 17HS4401 on the
TMC2209 at 24 V" (`docs/research/2026-11-21-speed-limit.md` on the
local branch `research/speed-limit`). Thus the two motors compare
directly.

## Answer

- **The 42HS60 is stronger at low speed, but it is slower.** Its
  voltage corner is at approximately **330 motor rpm (113 µs for each
  step, 22 rpm at the driven gear)**. The corner of the 17HS4401 is at
  approximately 660 motor rpm (57 µs). The higher inductance (6 mH) and
  the higher back-EMF constant of the 42HS60 cause this.
- **Below the corner, the 42HS60 has 1.7 times the torque of the
  17HS4401.** At 150 µs, the model gives 38.5 N·cm against 22.6 N·cm.
- **The two motors have the same torque at approximately 80 µs.** At
  intervals shorter than 80 µs, the 17HS4401 is stronger. At 60 µs, the
  42HS60 has only 37% of the torque of the 17HS4401.
- **Expected safe top speed of the 42HS60: 90–100 µs for each step
  (375–417 motor rpm, 25–28 rpm at the driven gear).** At 4 kg, the
  modeled torque is 4.9–5.8 times the load.
- **Expected stall zone: 60 µs and faster.** At 60 µs and 4 kg, the
  margin is only 1.3. At approximately 55 µs, the back-EMF alone is
  equal to the supply voltage. Thus the torque goes to zero.
- **One Vref can serve the two motors.** Set Vref to 1.92 V on the rig.
  Use `IRUN = 31` for the 17HS4401 (1.36 A RMS). Use `IRUN = 27` for the
  42HS60 (1.19 A RMS, 79% of its rating). `IRUN = 27` is in the range
  16–31 that the TMC2209 datasheet recommends.
- **Use `IHOLD = 16` for the two motors** (0.72 A RMS). The hold torque
  of the 42HS60 is then approximately 0.33 N·m. The 5 kg weight needs
  approximately 0.05 N·m at the motor.
- **The rig must sweep the 42HS60 from 200 µs down to 50 µs.** Refer to
  [Sweep range for the rig](#sweep-range-for-the-rig).

The torque values are a model, not a measurement. No source publishes a
24 V pull-out curve for this motor. The rig must measure the real limit.

**A result for the fleet.** [HARDWARE.md](../../HARDWARE.md#cruise_us-is-fleet-wide-but-the-motors-are-not)
says that the 17HS4401 is the weaker motor and thus sets the speed limit
of the fleet. The model does not agree for speeds above approximately
80 µs. The 42HS60 drives the heavier blinds, and it loses torque at a
lower speed. Thus the 42HS60 can set the limit of the fleet. The rig
must confirm this.

## Conversion from step interval to rpm

The conversion is the same for the two motors. Both have 200 full steps
for each revolution. At 1/8 microstep, the motor needs 1600 steps for
each revolution. The 1:15 gearbox gives 24 000 steps for each output
revolution.

```
motor rpm       = 60 000 000 / (interval_us × 1600) = 37 500 / interval_us
driven-gear rpm = motor rpm / 15                    =  2 500 / interval_us
```

## Motor data

### Part number

The repository gives two spellings of the part number:

- Issue #25: `42HS60-1504JA05-D20-03`.
- [HARDWARE.md](../../HARDWARE.md#bill-of-materials): `2HS60-1504JA05-020-03`.

The two names are almost certainly the same motor. The HARDWARE.md
spelling does not have the first "4", and it has "020" for "D20". Read
the label on the motor, and correct the wrong spelling.

The name follows the usual Chinese code for hybrid stepper motors:

| Field | Meaning | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| `42` | 42 mm frame (NEMA 17) | Sure. All vendors use it. |
| `HS` | Hybrid stepper, 1.8° | Sure. |
| `60` | Body length 60 mm | Sure. |
| `15` | Rated current 1.5 A/phase | Sure. |
| `04` | 4 leads (2 coils, bipolar) | Sure. |
| `JA05` | Vendor option code. A DEWO part with the same pattern exists (`42HS48-0404JA05-D24`). | Not known. Possibly the lead or connector type. |
| `D20` | Possibly a D-cut shaft with a length of 20 mm. A DEWO variant `42HS60-1504A05-D24-07` has `D24`. The usual shaft length is 24 mm. | Not confirmed. Measure the shaft. |
| `-03` | Vendor variant number | Not known. |

No source explains the `JA05`, `D20` and `-03` fields. The table marks
these values as an inference.

## Torque model

The model is the same as in the 17HS4401 research.

**1. Voltage limit (physics).** The TMC2209 datasheet (§6.4) gives the
back-EMF constant as `CBEMF = HoldingTorque / (2 × ICOILNOM)` (RMS for
each coil). For the 42HS60, this is 0.69 / 3.0 = 0.229 V/(rad/s) RMS,
or 0.323 V/(rad/s) peak. The 17HS4401 has 0.118 V/(rad/s) RMS. The
highest coil voltage in StealthChop is `VM × 248/256` ≈ 23.3 V peak.
The model finds the largest peak current that satisfies:

```
(I·R + E)² + (ωe·L·I)² ≤ Vmax²       E = CBEMF_peak × ωm,  ωe = 50 × ωm
```

The model then clips the current to the peak current that Vref and
`IRUN` set. The torque is the holding torque × (run current / rated
current) × the derate factor.

**2. Real losses.** The model applies the same derate factor as the
17HS4401 research: `d(rpm) = 0.76 − 0.000322 × (rpm − 90)`. This factor
comes from the measured 24 V curve of the 17HS16-2004S (a 40 mm motor).
It is less valid for the 42HS60. The 42HS60 has a longer stack, and
thus more iron loss at the same speed. Thus the real torque above the
corner can be lower than the table.

**Check of the method.** The script that made this document gives the
same 17HS4401 values as the earlier research (within 0.1 N·cm). The
small difference comes from the exact Vref formula.

### Current settings in the model

The model uses Vref 1.92 V for the two motors. The BTT formula of
HARDWARE.md, with the `IRUN` term of the TMC2209 datasheet (§9), is:

```
Irms = (IRUN + 1)/32 × 0.325 V / (0.11 Ω + 0.02 Ω) × 1/√2 × (Vref / 2.5 V)
```

| `IRUN` | Irms at Vref 1.92 V | 17HS4401 (1.7 A) | 42HS60 (1.5 A) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 31 | 1.36 A | 80% | 91% |
| 28 | 1.23 A | 72% | 82% |
| **27** | **1.19 A** | 70% | **79%** |
| 26 | 1.15 A | 67% | 76% |
| 16 (`IHOLD`) | 0.72 A | 42% | 48% |

At 55 µs (687 motor rpm), the back-EMF alone is equal to Vmax. The
model gives no torque at a higher speed. A real motor keeps a small
torque, but do not trust it.

**Effect of `IRUN = 31`.** Below the corner, the torque increases by
14% (44.0 N·cm at 150 µs). The corner moves to 126 µs (297 motor rpm).
Above the corner, the torque does not change, because the supply
voltage limits the current. Thus a higher `IRUN` does not make the
42HS60 faster.

**Sensitivity.** The inductance has a tolerance of ±20%. At 7.2 mH, the
corner moves to 128 µs. A warm coil (R + 30%) moves the corner from
113 µs to 118 µs. The two together move the corner to 134 µs. The
holding torque of 5.2 kg·cm in one listing moves the corner to 104 µs.

How to read the table:

- The model does not include the torque for acceleration, the friction
  of the gearbox bearings, or the friction of the cord. The ramp also
  adds a load. Thus a margin below approximately 2 is a stall risk.
- At 150 µs and slower, the 42HS60 has approximately 1.7 times the

- The holding torque percentages in issue #18 (3–13%) do not show the
  margin at speed. This table shows it.

## Driver settings

### Facts from the TMC2209 datasheet and the BTT module

- The BTT TMC2209 V1.3 has 0.11 Ω sense resistors (BTT manual, refer
  to [HARDWARE.md](../../HARDWARE.md#setting-vref-current-limit)).
- `IRUN` and `IHOLD` have 32 steps (0–31). The current is
  `(CS + 1)/32` of the full-scale current (datasheet §9, register
  `IHOLD_IRUN` in §5.2).
- With `I_scale_analog = 1` (the reset default of `GCONF`), Vref scales
  the full-scale current. Thus `IRUN` scales the current that Vref sets
  (datasheet §9.1).
- The register description of `IRUN` says: "Choose sense resistors in a
  way, that normal IRUN is 16 to 31 for best microstep performance"
  (§5.2). `IRUN = 27` is in this range.
- `CHOPCONF.vsense` must stay 0. The reset value is 0. With
  `vsense = 1`, the full-scale voltage goes from 0.325 V to 0.180 V. The
  current then decreases to 55%. The firmware writes all the registers
  at each boot (issue #20). Thus make sure that the `CHOPCONF` value
  keeps `vsense = 0`.
- The `IHOLD_IRUN` reset value has `IRUN = 31` and `IHOLD = 16`.

This research could not download the datasheet again on 2026-11-21.
The analog.com server did not answer. The section numbers come from rev
1.09, as in the 17HS4401 research.

### Can one Vref serve the two motors?

Yes. Vref 1.92 V gives the 80% target of the 17HS4401 at `IRUN = 31`.
The 42HS60 gets 79% at `IRUN = 27`. The current changes in steps of
1/32 (approximately 0.042 A at this Vref). Thus `IRUN` sets the 42HS60
current to within 1% of the target.

The opposite is not possible. Vref 1.69 V gives only 1.20 A at
`IRUN = 31`. The 17HS4401 needs 1.36 A. `IRUN` cannot go above 31. Thus
the rig must use the higher Vref.

**A failure of the UART is safe.** If the UART write does not occur, the
driver uses the reset value `IRUN = 31`. The 42HS60 then gets 1.36 A.
This is 91% of its rating, and it is not more than the rating. The
firmware also stops the test when the read-back fails (issue #20).

### Hold current

`IHOLD = 16` gives 0.72 A RMS at Vref 1.92 V. This is the same current
for the two motors.

- 42HS60: 48% of the rating, approximately 0.33 N·m of hold torque.
- 17HS4401: 42% of the rating, approximately 0.17 N·m of hold torque.
- The 5 kg weight gives approximately 0.05 N·m at the motor (issue #20).
  Thus the two motors have a margin of 3 or more.

The 42HS60 has more thermal mass than the 17HS4401. At the same
current, it becomes warm more slowly.

## Expected safe top speed

- **42HS60: 90–100 µs for each step** (375–417 motor rpm, **25–28 rpm
  at the driven gear**). At 4 kg, the margin is 4.9–5.8 (nominal) and
  4.0–4.8 with a warm coil and +20% inductance. At 5 kg, the margin is
  3.9–4.7.
- **42HS60 stall zone: 60 µs and faster** (more than 625 motor rpm,
  more than 42 rpm at the driven gear).
- **17HS4401 (from the earlier research): 70–80 µs** (31–36 rpm at the
  driven gear). Stall zone 50–57 µs and faster.
- **Do not use the present floor `RAMP_MIN_CRUISE_US = 60` with a
  42HS60 under a heavy load.** The model gives a margin of only 1.1–1.3
  at 4–5 kg.

## Recommendation for the rig

### Settings for each motor

| Setting | 17HS4401 | 42HS60-1504JA05-D20-03 |
|---|---|---|
| Vref | 1.92 V | 1.92 V |
| `I_scale_analog` | 1 | 1 |
| `vsense` | 0 | 0 |
| `IRUN` | 31 (1.36 A RMS, 80%) | **27** (1.19 A RMS, 79%) |
| `IHOLD` | 16 (0.72 A RMS) | 16 (0.72 A RMS) |
| `TPOWERDOWN`, `IHOLDDELAY` | As in issue #20 | As in issue #20 |
| Chopper mode | StealthChop2, `TPWMTHRS = 0` | StealthChop2, `TPWMTHRS = 0` |
| Motor cable | The 17HS4401 cable | The 42HS60 cable (the lead sequence is different) |

Make `IRUN` a parameter of the motor type in the rig firmware. Do not
change the trimpot when you change the motor. Before you change the
motor, remove the power from VM.

### Sweep range for the rig

Do these steps for the 42HS60:

1. Measure the phase resistance of the two coils. Record the values.
2. Do the first sweep in StealthChop2 (`SPREAD` at GND, `TPWMTHRS = 0`).
3. Start at 300 µs (the jog speed) as a baseline.
4. Go from 200 µs to 100 µs in steps of 10 µs.
5. Go from 100 µs to 50 µs in steps of 5 µs.
6. Stop the sweep at the first interval that loses steps. Then do the
   next weight.
7. Do the sweep at 1 kg, 2.5 kg and 4 kg. Do the 5 kg stress test last.
8. Do the same sweep in SpreadCycle (the test parameter of issue #20).
   Compare the stall intervals.

This range is **8.3 to 50 rpm at the driven gear** (125 to 750 motor
rpm). It includes the modeled corner (113 µs) and the modeled zero
torque (55 µs).

For the 17HS4401, keep the sweep of the earlier research: 150 µs down
to 40 µs (16.7 to 62.5 rpm at the driven gear).

To compare the two motors at the same points, use a common grid. Use
300, 200, 150, 120, 100, 90, 80, 70, 60 and 50 µs for the two motors.
Add 45 and 40 µs for the 17HS4401 only.

Before you start, do these steps:

- Lower `RAMP_MIN_CRUISE_US` for the rig environment only. The present
  floor of 60 µs blocks the sweep below 60 µs.
- Supply 24 V at the driver. A lower VM moves the corner to a lower
  speed. The 42HS60 is more sensitive to this than the 17HS4401.
- Make sure that `IRUN` agrees with the installed motor. Read it back
  through the UART.
- Record the real STEP period with a logic analyzer at each interval.

## Open points

- No manufacturer data sheet exists online for the exact part number.
  The R, L and holding torque come from vendor sources for the base
  model `42HS60-1504`.
- The fields `JA05`, `D20` and `-03` are not decoded.
- No 24 V pull-out curve exists for the 42HS60. The derate factor comes
  from a 40 mm motor. It is less valid for a 60 mm motor.
- The gearbox efficiency of 80% is an assumption.
- The result that the 42HS60 can set the speed limit of the fleet
  changes the statement in HARDWARE.md. Change HARDWARE.md only after
  the rig confirms it.
